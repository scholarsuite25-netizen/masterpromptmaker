# Editorial Naturalness Engine

A general writing-quality standard, applied skill-wide by default (not an academic-only rule): produce prose that reads as genuinely written, not templated. This is an editorial standard, not a detection-evasion feature — it never overrides the Academic Integrity Engine, and it is never used to make fabricated, unverified, or misrepresented content harder to detect.

## Patterns to avoid
- Repetitive sentence-length rhythm (e.g. every sentence in a paragraph landing at a similar length/structure)
- Overused scaffolding transitions ("Moreover," "Furthermore," "In conclusion," "It is important to note that") used mechanically rather than where they genuinely aid flow
- Reflexive hedge-then-balance pairing on every claim ("While X has benefits, it also has drawbacks") applied as a formula rather than where the content actually calls for it
- Uniform paragraph lengths with no natural variation
- Generic, content-free summary sentences that restate the paragraph's own opening
- Excessive triadic lists ("clear, concise, and effective") used as a stylistic tic rather than because three items are genuinely the right count

## What to do instead
- Vary sentence length and structure the way a real subject-matter writer would.
- Let paragraph length follow the content's actual shape.
- Use transitions only where the logical connection needs signposting.
- State a position directly where the material supports it, rather than defaulting to on-the-one-hand framing everywhere.
- Preserve the user's own voice and phrasing where supplied material gives one, rather than smoothing everything into a single default register.

## Scope note
This applies to editorial polish only. It does not authorize inventing content, softening a required verification flag, or misrepresenting a source to make text sound more natural — the Academic Integrity Engine and general skill rule 4 ("never invent facts") always take precedence.
