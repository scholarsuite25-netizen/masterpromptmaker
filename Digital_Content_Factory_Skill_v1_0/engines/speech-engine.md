# Speech, Keynote & Convocation Address Engine

For `/create-speech`. Distinct from Course/Lecture (which teaches a skill or concept) and Proposal (which persuades toward a business decision): this engine produces spoken-delivery content for institutional and ceremonial occasions.

## Occasion types (select one)
- **Convocation/matriculation address** — ceremonial, formal, addressed to graduands/new students and their families
- **Welcome/opening remarks** — short, sets tone for an event, typically 3-7 minutes
- **Keynote address** — the substantive centerpiece of an event, develops a small number of ideas in depth
- **Vote of thanks / closing remarks** — brief, appreciative, names contributors accurately
- **Other institutional address** — ask what the occasion requires rather than forcing it into the above

## Structure
1. Opening — audience acknowledgment (protocol order confirmed with the user, never guessed for formal/ceremonial settings), a hook appropriate to the occasion
2. A small number of core points (typically 3, rarely more) — spoken content needs far less density than written content; do not compress an essay into a speech
3. Illustrative material — stories, examples, or data the user supplies; never a fabricated anecdote or statistic presented as fact
4. A clear, memorable closing line — the part most likely to be quoted or remembered
5. Delivery notes: estimated speaking time (~130-150 words/minute as a planning guide), pacing/pause cues, emphasis cues on key lines

## Protocol and accuracy
- Confirm the correct protocol order of dignitaries/titles to acknowledge before drafting an opening for a formal institutional occasion — never guess titles, names, or seniority order.
- Never fabricate a quotation, statistic, or biographical fact attributed to the speaker or anyone mentioned in the speech.

## Never
- Write dense, paragraph-heavy prose meant to be read rather than heard — vary sentence length for spoken rhythm, favor shorter sentences at emphasis points.
- Invent a dignitary's name, title, or achievement.

Apply the Editorial Naturalness Engine's prose standard and the Global-Audience Engine where the occasion has an international or mixed audience.
